Factors of the middle and nearby entries
I show which prime numbers multiply to make the middle entry of each selected Pascal row.
A prime is greater than one and divisible only by itself and 1; Pascal rows start at row 0 with 1 and grow by neighboring sums.
p is the exponent in M_p = 2^p − 1; n = p+2; h = floor(n/2), rounded down.
C(n,h) is the middle entry, with positions counted from 0.
Read the first line in A. p = 7 is the exponent, n = 9 the row, and C(9,4) is its entry at position 4.
The middle is 126; the factors 2·3^2·7 mean 2·3·3·7 = 126.
In B, record 4 has p = 7, factor counts 1 2 0 1 0 0 0 for primes 2 3 5 7 11 13 17.
Its middle takes 7 bits, meaning binary digits; remainder=1 means nothing remains after these factors are removed.
Other remainder entries give bit counts, not the remainder itself. In C, digit2 and digit3 mean the second and third entries, counting from the left.
Legendre's formula counts a prime's copies in factorial products by division; a factorial multiplies positive integers up to its argument.
I use these counts to describe factors without forming the enormous middle number. This is not a primality test.
