Middle entries for the first fifty listed exponents
I give each middle entry as a number, its prime factors, and its base eight writing.
Pascal rows have 1 at the ends and neighboring sums inside; row 0 is just 1.
p is the exponent in M_p = 2^p − 1; n = p+2 is the row; h = floor(n/2) is its middle position.
C(n,h) means that entry, counting positions from 0; floor means round down.
Read record #4. Its list number is 4, p = 7, n = 9, h = 4, and C(9,4) names the middle entry.
Decimal gives 126; octal, meaning base 8, gives 176_8 = 1·64 + 7·8 + 6 = 126.
The factorization 2·3^2·7 equals 126. A prime is greater than one and divisible only by itself and 1.
For p <= 127 I give the whole value and factorization. Larger records give decimal, binary and base eight digit counts.
They also give the factors through 17 and the bit count of the remaining product, whose prime factors are at least 19.
The base eight preview keeps the first and last 12 digits; the number in the gap counts omitted digits.
I use these forms to compare size and factors without printing millions of digits. They do not prove primality.
